Options and Hedging Strategies
Definition of Options
Option: A contract that gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset at a predetermined price on or before a certain date.

Call Option: Gives the holder the right to buy an asset.

Put Option: Gives the holder the right to sell an asset.

Example of Stock Options
Companies, especially tech and startups, often compensate employees with stock options (ESOPs) instead of high salaries.

Vesting Period: ESOPs generally have a vesting period after which the options can be exercised.

Analogies
Car Insurance as Put Option: When the value of a car depreciates due to an accident, insurance can be claimed, similar to exercising a put option when the asset value falls.
Swaps
Interest Rate Swaps
Interest Rate Swap: A derivative contract through which two parties exchange cash flows of one party's financial instrument for those of the other party's financial instrument. These are most often used to exchange a fixed interest rate for a floating rate or vice versa.

Allows holders of a fixed-rate loan to swap their interest payments with those of a floating-rate payer to potentially lower interest costs and hedge against rising rates.

Example ...
Assume a loan with a fixed interest rate: Bank executes a swap for you to pay at floating market rates and receive fixed rates, thus managing cost if market rates adjust unfavorably.

Securitization and Credit Default Swaps
Securitization
Securitization: The process of pooling various types of debt and selling them as consolidated financial instruments to investors.

Example: Mortgage-backed securities (MBS) allow banks to turn the mortgage loans into liquid securities.

Credit Default Swaps (CDS)
Credit Default Swap: A financial derivative that allows an investor to "swap" or offset their credit risk with that of another investor.

Primarily serves as a form of insurance against the default of a borrower.

Example of CDS
A bank holding a risky loan might buy a CDS from another financial institution to insure against the borrower defaulting.
Real-Life Applications of Derivatives
Business Expansion Strategies
Companies like Starbucks may use a "call option" strategy for expansion, starting with a single location to test the market before expanding further.
Pharmaceutical Development
Pharmaceutical companies use a "real options" framework in drug development stages to decide investment continuation based on stage utility or failure.
